# AI Use Case Library

Ideas to pull from during an [[ai-audit-process]] and when scoping done-for-you builds ([[mirk-pricing-ladder]]). Filtered for [[mirk-icp]]: owner-led local businesses where admin and follow-up are the pain. Main source: Alex Lieberman's list of 103 agent use cases ([[103 AI Use Cases - Alex Lieberman]]); numbers in brackets point to the item there.

Pattern behind almost all of them: **watch a source on a schedule → notice a change → draft the next action for a human to approve.** That framing is easy to explain to owners and maps to the concierge's audit → optimize → automate loop.

## Any client (lead with these)
- **Free-text attribution** [6]: read every "how did you hear about us?" answer from forms and calls, clean it up, group by source monthly. Shows owners which referral partners and channels actually drive work.
- **Repeated-question mining** [7, 14]: group questions from chat, email, texts and call transcripts; check whether the website answers each. Output: FAQ and website copy gaps ranked by frequency.
- **Lost-quote revival** [47]: monthly recheck of lost quotes or deals against what changed (season, new budget, new decision-maker); draft a note for the ones worth reopening.
- **No-show rescue** [55]: within 10 minutes of a missed appointment, send two new times plus a short personal message.
- **Quote before hang-up** [52]: listen for scope on the call and draft the estimate from the price book so it's ready to approve at the end.
- **Promise tracking** [56, 95]: pull every "I'll send that over" from meeting transcripts and email; nudge before it goes stale.
- **Pre-meeting refresher** [94]: brief before each client meeting from past emails, texts and notes.
- **Calendar vs. priorities** [57]: tag the owner's week against stated priorities. A strong opener for the "owner stuck in ops" buying trigger.
- **Hidden load-bearing people** [58]: find who answers most internal questions. Key-person risk, and the best first interview in an assessment.

## By industry
- **Home services** (HVAC, plumbing, roofing): maintenance schedules from customers' equipment model numbers, with filter and tune-up reminders [102]; customer year-in-review for maintenance-plan members [9]; off-market and long-held-owner signals from public records for roofing and remodel outreach [101] (see [[hyperagent]]).
- **Insurance and professional services** (law, accounting): relationship-strength scores built from email, calendar and texts, with alerts when key clients go cold [97]; handwritten-card follow-up when an ideal prospect engages [8]; prefilled vendor and security questionnaires from past answers [54].
- **Clinics** (PT, dental): match bills against insurer explanations of benefits and draft appeals for denials [69]; voice agent that waits on hold with insurers or calls offices to confirm details [76, 91]. Keep PHI out unless compliance is handled ([[mirk-icp]] disqualifies heavy compliance work; see [[dentimax]]).
- **B2B-serving clients**: watch customers' job postings for upsell or churn signals [48]; mine competitors' 1–2 star reviews for positioning [36].
- **Hiring clients**: job descriptions drafted from what the top performer actually does [67]; transcript scoring against an interview rubric [63, 68].

## For MIRK itself
- **AI-answer share tracking** [3]: regularly ask ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity the questions local owners ask ("AI consultant Mesa", "AI receptionist for small business") and log whether MIRK appears. Pairs with the site's SEO pages.
- **Ideal-customer engagement alerts** [5] and **fast comments** [13]: alert when a target owner engages with or posts about Ian's topic; draft a reply.
- **Inbox-to-post mining** [14]: turn questions answered 3+ times into posts ([[short-form-content]]).
- **Lessons from calls** [99]: turn discovery and concierge call transcripts into a running playbook of patterns and quotes.

## How to use in an assessment
1. Map the client's processes first; pick 2–3 items above that match the biggest time drains.
2. Frame each as source → trigger → drafted action → human approves.
3. Estimate hours saved and build effort before recommending; most of these are done-for-you builds or concierge sessions.
